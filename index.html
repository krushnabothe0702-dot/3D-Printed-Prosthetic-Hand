3D-Printed Prosthetic Hand — a body-powered, cord-driven prototype

A student prototype of a low-cost mechanical hand. Bending the wrist pulls cords (artificial tendons) that curl the fingers; elastic on the back of each finger opens them again. No motors, no batteries. All parts have been sliced, 3D-printed and assembled, and the hand has picked up a roll of tape in a first tabletop test. Measured testing is next, so no grip-strength results are claimed yet.

SEC 02

Why we built it

Prosthetic hands can be expensive and hard to access. Open, community-driven 3D-printed hands have shown that a simple mechanical hand can be made from plastic parts, cord and elastic on an ordinary desktop printer.

We wanted to understand that approach by doing it ourselves: start from a hand-drawn sketch with measurements, turn it into printable parts, slice and print them, then assemble the joints and route the tendons by hand. The aim is learning and a working bench prototype — not a medical device.

The question we are answering

Can a student team build a low-cost, body-powered hand from 3D-printed parts and cord, in which bending the wrist reliably closes all five fingers and releasing it opens them again?

SEC 03

How it is meant to work

1

Pull cord (tendon)

A cord runs from each fingertip, along the palm side of the finger, through the palm, to an anchor on the gauntlet.

2

Hinged joints

Each finger is made of printed segments joined by pins. The cord pulls the segments so they pivot and the finger curls.

3

Wrist drive

The hand pivots on the gauntlet at the wrist. Bending the wrist increases the cord path length, so the cords tighten and the fingers close.

4

Elastic return

Red elastic on the back of each finger pulls it straight again when the wrist is relaxed and the cords go slack.

SEC 04

Engineering journey

How the prototype was built, in order, with our real photos and videos. Click a photo to enlarge it.

  1. Step 01 · Sketch

    Hand sketch with measurements

    We started with a hand-drawn sketch of the palm, fingers and a forearm section, with rough measurements (see Design). We then used it to propose printable dimensions for the palm, finger segments, thumb and hinge holes.

  2. Step 02 · Slicing

    Laying out every part on the build plate

    All parts — palm, finger segments, thumb, gauntlet and the small pins — were arranged and sliced in the slicer software before printing.

  3. Step 03 · Printing

    3D printing the parts

    The parts were printed in white filament on an enclosed FDM 3D printer, with support material under the overhanging parts.

  4. Step 04 · Assembly

    Joints and tendon routing

    After removing supports, the finger segments were pinned together and the tendon cords were threaded through each finger and the palm.

  5. Step 05 · Tensioning

    Tying off and tensioning at the gauntlet

    The cords were brought out at the gauntlet and tied off. Each finger needs its own tension so that all fingers close together when the wrist bends.

  6. Step 06 · Assembled

    Complete prototype

    Palm, four fingers, thumb and gauntlet are assembled, with tendons on the palm side and red elastic returns on the back of the fingers.

  7. Step 07 · Joint check

    Checking the finger joints

    Before testing a grasp, each finger was flexed by hand to check that the joints swing freely and the cords and return elastics move without catching.

  8. Step 08 · First grasp test

    Picking up a roll of tape

    A first tabletop test: the hand, operated by hand on the bench (not worn), closed its fingers around a roll of tape, picked it up and held it. This shows the cord mechanism can grip a light object. Grip force, wrist angle and repeatability have not been measured yet.

SEC 05

Design and dimensions

Two sets of numbers: the rough measurements from our original sketch, and the proposed starting dimensions for printable parts. These are design values; the as-built hand has not been measured yet.

From the original sketch
Forearm section250 mm
Palm height90 mm
Palm width64 mm
Finger length (as drawn)200 mm
Finger spread / height100 mm
Proposed printable dimensions
Palm (W × H × T)64 × 90 × 12 mm
Finger width16 mm
Finger proximal segment55 mm
Finger distal segment35 mm
Total finger reach90 mm
Thumb segments38 mm each
Hinge holes⌀4.3 mm
Forearm attachment250 mm, separate part

Why the finger length changed

The sketch reads as 200 mm fingers, which is far longer than a real hand. The printable starting point uses about 90 mm of finger reach (55 + 35 mm segments) so the hand stays in human proportion.

Printed parts

Palm, finger segments, thumb, gauntlet and pins, in white filament (FDM).

Tendons

Copper-coloured braided cord, one per finger and one for the thumb.

Return elastics

Red elastic on the back of each finger to reopen the hand.

Wrist hinge

Pins join the palm to the gauntlet so the wrist can drive the tendons.

Safety note

This is an educational, non-clinical prototype. It has not been load-tested, fitted or reviewed by a prosthetist. Do not use it for heavy loads or wear it on a limb without review by a qualified clinician or engineer.
